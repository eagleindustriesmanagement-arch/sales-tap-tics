# Sales Taptics: homepage research (ROI, credibility, conversion, pricing)

Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the network proxy for almost every domain (hbr.org, gong.io, salesmanagement.org, csoinsights.com, huthwaiteinternational.com, highspot/brainshark PDFs, globenewswire). Every figure below comes from search-engine snippets of the named page or from secondary sources that quote it. **Before anything goes live, open the primary URL and confirm the exact wording.** Nothing here is invented; anything I could not trace is marked as unverified.

Reliability scale: **A** = primary study (peer-reviewed or published experiment with a method); **B** = reputable analyst or association report (survey or member data, method only partly disclosed); **C** = widely repeated, primary source not found or not checkable.

**Flagged, do not use on the homepage:**
- **"353% ROI / $4.53 per $1" (often attributed to Huthwaite or Accenture).** Search traces it to an Accenture report ("Running Training Like a Business," year unclear). It concerns general corporate training, not sales. I could not find a primary document. **C**.
- **"218% higher income per employee" (ATD/ASTD).** It probably comes from ASTD's *Profiting from Learning* (Bassi, Ludwig, McMurrer & Van Buren, 2000: 575 publicly traded U.S. firms, 1996–98 data). That study's verified headline is about shareholder return (top-half trainers had 86% higher TSR than the bottom half), and I could not confirm the 218% figure in it. Other problems: firm-level, correlational, about 25 years old, not about sales. **C**.
- **"50% higher net sales per employee" from continuous training.** Blogs attribute it to Sales Performance International, but there is no primary study. **C**.
- **"84% forgotten after 90 days" (SPI) / "87% lost in 12 weeks" (Xerox) / "ES Research training decay."** These circulate without a traceable primary study. **C**.
- **Gartner "coached reps 19% more likely to hit quota" and "76% hit quota with weekly coaching."** These appear only in blog roundups. **C**.
- **Second Nature's "close 46% more deals" and Gong's "77% more revenue per rep using AI."** These are vendor claims about the vendor's own product. Don't borrow them.

**Recommendation: headline**
> **Trained sales floors sold 12% more per day.**
> *Attribution line:* "Stores where both sales associates and managers were trained saw daily sales rise 12.1% vs. randomly selected untrained stores. Prada, Rucci & Urzúa, randomized field experiment at a large retailer, Inter-American Development Bank / IZA Discussion Paper 12447 (2019)."

Why this one: it is the only figure here from a **randomized** comparison that measures **actual sales on a retail floor**, which is the closest analogue to a dealership showroom. Two caveats must stay in the attribution: the training was leadership and communication skills, and it was not Sales Taptics. Don't write "Sales Taptics users sell 12% more."

**Fallback**, if legal or the team finds a non-sales-tactic soft-skills study too indirect:
> **Great coaching lifts core reps' performance by up to 19%.**
> *Attribution line:* "Reps in the middle 60% who received the best coaching performed up to 19% better against goal than those who received the worst. Dixon & Adamson, CEB Sales Executive Council, Harvard Business Review, 2011."

This one is the most widely recognised, and Sales Taptics' practice-plus-scoring loop is closer to coaching than to classroom training. The figure is a best-versus-worst comparison, so keep "up to."

## 2. Credibility claims about the research base

Verified scale figures (from search snippets of each source):
- **Rackham / Huthwaite (SPIN Selling, 1988):** 35,000+ sales calls observed over 12 years in 20+ countries (Huthwaite says 23), research team of about 30, budget of about $1M. Source: huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-methodology plus secondary summaries. 35,000 is confirmed.
- **Dixon & McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (Portfolio/Penguin, 2022):** analysis of **more than 2.5 million recorded sales calls** with Tethr's data science team. Finding: 40–60% of qualified deals are lost to "no decision." Sources: jolteffect.com; Tethr press release (prnewswire.com/news-releases/tethr-announces-joltrx-...-301626831.html).
- **Gong Labs:** published studies on **300M+ cold calls** ("The hidden power of cold calling: Insights from 300M calls," gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls), **24M sales calls** (gong.io/resources/labs/selling-is-more-complex-than-ever-and-24m-sales-calls-told-us-why/), **326K calls** (talk-to-listen study), **519K discovery calls**, and an early study of ~25,537 calls. Gong says its models are trained on "more than three billion customer interactions." That figure is proprietary model-training data (calls, emails, meetings), not published research, so **don't cite it**.
- **Choice-overload meta-analyses:** Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010, *J. Consumer Research*): 63 conditions from 50 experiments, N = 5,036, mean effect about zero with large variance. Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015, *J. Consumer Psychology*): 99 observations, N = 7,202, identifies when overload occurs. Use this honestly: choice overload is *conditional*.
- **Galinsky & Mussweiler (2001, *JPSP* 81:657–669):** in their experiments, first-offer extremity explained up to ~50% of variance in outcomes.

**Safe phrasings** (true only if the library really draws on the studies named):
1. "Every technique traces to published research, from Neil Rackham's 35,000 observed sales calls to studies of millions of recorded sales conversations." *(Supported by Rackham, JOLT 2.5M, Gong 24M / 300M.)*
2. "Built on research spanning millions of recorded sales conversations." *(Supported. Footnote JOLT 2.5M+ and Gong Labs 24M+.)*
3. "Grounded in 40 years of sales and negotiation research: SPIN, Gong Labs, the JOLT study, Harvard and Kellogg negotiation science." *(Use the institutions only if the specific papers are actually cited in-app.)*

**"Millions of hours" is not supportable.** No source reports hours, and multiplying call counts by an assumed length would be an invented number. Use **"millions of recorded sales conversations"** instead. Avoid "hundreds of millions" unless the 300M cold-call study is really a source for specific techniques. Also avoid implying that Sales Taptics analysed these calls. Write "research spanning," not "we analysed."

## 3. What makes sales-training / B2B SaaS homepages convert to trials

1. **State the value in under 10 seconds.** NN/g found the first 10 seconds decide whether a visitor stays or leaves, and that a clear value proposition is how a page earns longer attention (nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/). *Taptics:* headline = outcome ("Practice the close before the customer walks in"). Subhead = mechanism (learn a tactic, practice against an AI customer, get scored, get certified).
2. **One primary CTA plus one low-commitment secondary.** Hyperbound uses "Book a demo" plus "Try a Roleplay" with "no credit card, no sales call required." Second Nature uses only "Book a demo" (hyperbound.ai; secondnature.ai). *Taptics:* primary "Start free"; secondary "Try a 2-minute roleplay," which runs immediately with no signup.
3. **Let them try the product on the page.** Navattic's vendor report finds interactive demos convert 12% better than product videos (navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2026; vendor data, so treat as directional). *Taptics:* an embedded live text roleplay ("customer says: I need to think about it") that returns a real score card. This is the strongest proof available without customers.
4. **Write at a 5th–7th grade reading level.** Unbounce's benchmark analysis found pages at that level converted at 11.1%, compared with 7.1% at 8th–9th grade and 5.3% at professional level (unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/). *Taptics:* write for a floor rep on a phone, not a VP of Enablement. Short words, no "revenue intelligence" jargon.
5. **Use real product visuals, not abstract art.** Show the actual scorecard (behaviors hit or missed), the certification badge and the manager team view. *Taptics:* three real screenshots, labelled. No mock dashboards with fake numbers.
6. **Social proof without logos: borrow credibility from research, honestly.** CXL warns that stock-photo or too-perfect testimonials backfire and read as fake (cxl.com/blog/is-social-proof-really-that-important/). *Taptics:* use a "The research behind it" strip that names Rackham, JOLT, Gong Labs and Galinsky with citations. Add a founder-origin line ("built on dealership floors"), and later add verifiable pilot quotes with name, store and role. No placeholder logos, no "trusted by 1,000+ reps" until it's true.
7. **Prefer precise, sourced numbers over round claims.** Research on "sharp" vs round numbers finds precise figures read as more factual (Schindler & Yalch, "It Seems Factual, But Is It?", researchgate.net/publication/279544736). *Taptics:* "12.1%" with the citation beats "up to 50% more sales."
8. **Remove signup friction.** Imagescape cut a form from 11 fields to 4 and saw +120% conversions (imagescape.com form_case_study.pdf). Expedia's removal of a single confusing "Company" field is reported as worth $12M per year (via Unbounce/HubSpot). *Taptics:* email or Google sign-in only. Ask about store, role and team size after the first roleplay.
9. **Self-serve trial for reps, a demo path for stores.** Category leaders (Second Nature, Mindtickle) gate everything behind demos. Hyperbound and Yoodli offer free tiers. A rep or a single-store manager wants to try tonight, while a dealer group wants a call. *Taptics:* "Start free" for Solo/Team and "Talk to us" for Dealership/Group.
10. **"Simple to use, deep to learn" as structure, not slogan.** Show the 4-step loop (Learn, See it, Practice, Certify) in one row. Then show depth: "120+ techniques across discovery, objections, negotiation and closing," with a browsable sample of 6. (This is a design judgment; I found no study measuring the phrase itself.)
11. **Give managers their own section.** The buyer (sales manager, GM) is not the user (rep). Add one block: "See who's practising, who's certified and which objections your team fumbles." *Taptics:* show a screenshot of the team view.
12. **Mobile first.** Unbounce reports that most SaaS landing-page traffic is mobile (per its SaaS benchmark page; verify the exact share). Floor reps live on phones, so the demo roleplay must work by voice on mobile.
13. **ROI calculator: use cautiously.** It is common in B2B, but I found no rigorous public evidence that it lifts trials. If used, make every input user-editable (gross per unit, units per month) and apply only the cited, conservative 12.1% or a user-chosen lift, with the assumption visible next to the result.

## 4. Pricing psychology and per-seat pricing for team products

**Psychology (academic basis):**
- **Three tiers / compromise effect:** people disproportionately pick the middle option, especially when they must justify the choice to someone else (Simonson 1989, *J. Consumer Research*), which is exactly a manager buying for a team. Price Intelligently's *SaaS Pricing Page Blueprint* (270 pricing pages) found most companies show 3–4 tiers. Claims that "3 tiers = +30% revenue" or "a 'Most Popular' badge = +38%" circulate without a primary source, so don't rely on them.
- **Anchoring:** the first number seen shapes judgment (Tversky & Kahneman 1974; Galinsky & Mussweiler 2001 for negotiation). *Taptics:* frame cost against one deal ("less than the gross on one car a month") and show the store plan so the per-rep price looks small.
- **Decoy / asymmetric dominance:** adding an option that is clearly worse than the target shifts choice toward the target (Huber, Payne & Puto 1982, *J. Consumer Research*). Caveat: the effect weakens or vanishes with realistic, perceptual or verbal options (Frederick, Lee & Baskin 2014, *J. Marketing Research*, "The Limits of Attraction"). Don't build a fake decoy tier. A real Solo tier already makes Team look like better value.
- **"Most popular" highlight:** a standard pattern, but only label a tier "most popular" once it actually is. Until then use "Recommended for teams," which is honest.
- **Annual discount:** "2 months free" (≈16.7%) is the common framing. One scan of SaaS pricing pages found a 25% median discount among those that state one (suffdigital.com, secondary). Observed norm: **15–25%**.
- **Trial norms:** secondary benchmarks put the typical B2B SaaS trial at 14 days, with shorter trials converting better than 30-day ones, and trials that require a card converting more but retaining worse (artisangrowthstrategies.com, ordwaylabs.com; secondary, unverified). AI roleplay competitors mostly use a **free tier** (Hyperbound, Yoodli) or **demo only** (Second Nature, Mindtickle) rather than a timed trial.

Pattern: enterprise AI roleplay tools hide prices and sell per seat with annual minimums. The self-serve players publish prices at $8–55 per seat per month. **Publishing prices is itself a differentiator** for dealership and SMB buyers.

**Recommended structure.** The ranges below are *observed market ranges*, not recommended final prices:
| Tier | Who | Includes | Billing model | Observed market range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo** | One rep paying for themselves | Full technique library, unlimited text plus capped voice roleplays, scoring, personal certificates | Per user, monthly or annual | ~$8–40/month (Yoodli Pro/Advanced, Exec Starter, PitchMonster) |
| **Team** ("Recommended for teams") | Manager plus ~3–15 reps | Everything in Solo, plus manager dashboard, team certifications, assign drills, custom customer personas, leaderboards | Per seat, with a small minimum (e.g. 3 seats) | ~$20–70/seat/month (Exec Pro, PitchMonster, Kendo, Second Nature and Hyperbound reports) |
| **Dealership / Enterprise** | Whole store or dealer group | Everything in Team, plus unlimited reps per rooftop, store-specific scenarios (inventory, F&I, trade-in), multi-store reporting, onboarding, SSO, priority support | **Flat per rooftop** (avoids seat juggling with floor turnover) or annual contract; "from $X" or talk to us | Video-library incumbents ~$99–400/month per store; AI roleplay enterprise deals reported at ~$15K–$42K+/yr |

Also offer an annual option at "2 months free." Show the Team tier in the middle with the honest "Recommended" badge. Publish a real "from" price on Dealership so the page anchors without hiding behind "contact sales."
